Practice 01

Statecraft, Governance & Institutional Transformation

Helping governments strengthen institutional capacity, design and implement reforms, and improve public-sector performance.

Ambitious agendas succeed or fail on the strength of the institutions that carry them. We work alongside leaders and their teams to turn political priorities into practical programmes, build the routines that keep delivery on track, and strengthen the capability of the public service over time.

Our role is to strengthen government, not to stand in for it. We leave behind clearer priorities, better information and people who are confident running the system themselves.

Typical engagements

  • Establishing and strengthening delivery units and centre-of-government functions
  • Designing reform programmes with clear priorities, sequencing and accountability
  • Institutional reviews and organisational redesign for ministries and agencies
  • Performance frameworks and routines that leaders actually use
  • Leadership advisory and capability building for senior officials
  • Policy development support in priority sectors

Who it’s for

  • Offices of heads of state and government
  • Ministries and public agencies
  • Development institutions supporting reform programmes

Questions we help answer

  1. Q1Which few priorities will define this term in office?
  2. Q2How will we know, week by week, whether delivery is on track?
  3. Q3What must the public service be able to do in five years that it cannot do today?
Practice 02

Nation Branding, Strategic Communications & Global Positioning

Supporting governments in strengthening their international reputation, soft power and global visibility.

A nation’s reputation shapes who invests, who visits, who partners and who listens. We help governments define a credible national story, align the institutions that tell it and position the country with confidence among investors, partners and international audiences.

Credibility comes from consistency between what a country says and what it does. That is why our positioning work is closely tied to reform and investment, not separate from it.

Typical engagements

  • National narrative and positioning strategy
  • Reputation audits and perception research among priority audiences
  • Strategic communications for reform programmes and national moments
  • International engagement plans for summits, forums and state visits
  • Coordination across investment, tourism and trade promotion agencies
  • Message and media preparation for senior leaders

Who it’s for

  • Presidencies and prime ministers’ offices
  • Ministries of foreign affairs, information and trade
  • Investment, tourism and export promotion agencies

Questions we help answer

  1. Q1What do the audiences that matter most believe about us today?
  2. Q2Which story can we tell credibly, and prove?
  3. Q3How do our institutions speak with one voice abroad?
Practice 03

Investment Diplomacy, Market Access & Strategic Partnerships

Helping international companies and investors navigate African markets, engage with governments and develop strategic investment and commercial partnerships.

Africa’s opportunities are real, but they rarely reward a purely transactional approach. We help investors and companies understand the political and institutional landscape, engage government with credibility and build partnerships that serve both commercial and national objectives.

We also work with governments on the other side of the table, helping them prepare investment-ready projects and present them with clarity.

Typical engagements

  • Market-entry strategy and political-economy analysis
  • Government engagement planning and stakeholder mapping
  • Preparing for and structuring high-level meetings with government
  • Partnership design between investors, governments and development institutions
  • Support for governments preparing investment-ready projects
  • Ongoing strategic counsel through the life of an investment

Who it’s for

  • International companies and investors
  • Development finance institutions
  • Governments seeking credible investment partners

Questions we help answer

  1. Q1Who really decides, and what do they need to see from us?
  2. Q2How do we align our commercial case with national priorities?
  3. Q3What does a partnership look like that both sides can defend in public?
