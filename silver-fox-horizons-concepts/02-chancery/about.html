Counsel for those who carry responsibility.

We are a strategic advisory firm working at the intersection of statecraft, international positioning and investment diplomacy.

§ IThe mandate

Why we exist.

Silver Fox Horizons partners with governments, institutions and businesses to strengthen state capacity, elevate global influence and unlock investment opportunities across Africa.

The questions that matter most to a nation rarely sit neatly in one discipline. A reform programme succeeds or stalls on how it is explained at home and abroad. A country’s reputation shapes the investment it attracts. And investment, well made, strengthens the very institutions that make reform possible.

We were founded to work across those connections: to bring the craft of government, the discipline of strategic communication and the practice of investment diplomacy into one conversation, in service of African ambitions and on African terms.

§ IIHow we work

An approach built on patience and candour.

  1. I.

    Listen first

    Every engagement begins with questions, not answers. We take time to understand the political context, the institutional history and what success would mean to the people we serve.

  2. II.

    Diagnose honestly

    We tell clients what we see, including what is uncomfortable. Candour delivered privately is one of the most valuable things an adviser can offer.

  3. III.

    Design with ownership

    Solutions are built with the teams who will carry them, so that capability stays in the institution and not with the adviser.

  4. IV.

    Deliver, and stay close

    We remain alongside our clients through implementation, adjusting course as circumstances change, and stepping back when the work can stand on its own.

What we hold to

i.

Discretion

Much of our work happens before anything is announced. We treat every confidence as a trust, and let our clients take the credit.

ii.

Rigour

Advice grounded in evidence, institutional knowledge and an honest reading of political realities, never in slogans.

iii.

Long horizons

We measure success in institutions and decades, not news cycles. Reforms and reputations should outlast a mandate.

iv.

African ownership

Strategies are shaped with, and owned by, the African leaders and institutions who will carry them forward.
