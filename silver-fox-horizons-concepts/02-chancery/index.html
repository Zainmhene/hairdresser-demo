Our position

§ IThe practices

Three practices, one conviction.

That strong institutions, a credible voice in the world and well-made partnerships reinforce one another.

I.

Statecraft, Governance & Institutional Transformation

We help governments strengthen institutional capacity, design and implement reforms, and improve public-sector performance. The work is practical and patient: clarifying priorities at the centre of government, building the machinery to deliver them, and helping institutions earn the confidence of the citizens they serve.

For

Heads of state’s offices, ministries and public institutions.

Read more about Statecraft & Governance
II.

Nation Branding, Strategic Communications & Global Positioning

We support governments in strengthening their international reputation, soft power and global visibility, so that a nation’s story is told with clarity and conviction by those with the most right to tell it.

For

Presidencies, ministries of foreign affairs, tourism and investment agencies.

Read more about Nation Branding & Positioning
III.

Investment Diplomacy, Market Access & Strategic Partnerships

We help international companies and investors navigate African markets, engage with governments and develop strategic investment and commercial partnerships that are built to last, and are good for the countries that host them.

For

International companies, investors and development institutions.

Read more about Investment Diplomacy
§ IIPrinciples

How we conduct ourselves.

Four commitments that shape every engagement, whoever the client and whatever the brief.

i.

Discretion

Much of our work happens before anything is announced. We treat every confidence as a trust, and let our clients take the credit.

ii.

Rigour

Advice grounded in evidence, institutional knowledge and an honest reading of political realities, never in slogans.

iii.

Long horizons

We measure success in institutions and decades, not news cycles. Reforms and reputations should outlast a mandate.

iv.

African ownership

Strategies are shaped with, and owned by, the African leaders and institutions who will carry them forward.
